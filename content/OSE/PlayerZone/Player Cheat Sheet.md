### How do I move through the Wilderness?
Refer to [[Hex Travel]] for speeds and other considerations. 

Generally:
1. Where are you going (roughly)? How fast are you moving? Are you taking time to forage/hunt?
2. Lost/wrong direction check. This may require player rolls. Hunting or foraging roll if applicable.
3. Encounter or Travel Event roll.
4. Mark off rations, and decide on camping.
5. Overnight Encounter check. 
6. Benefits of rest (Natural Healing, spells)
7. Repeat until you arrive, get lost, or die!
### How do I move through the Town?

1. Where are you going in Town? What are you doing? Laying low, listening for rumours, shopping, selling, hiring retainers?
2. Roll for town encounter or reaction, potentially. This is modified based on Heat.
3. Roleplay or Outcomes, which may involve reaction rolls.
4. Mark off town turn/gold
5. Adjust town heat as needed.
6. Repeat until 8 turns.

Town Heat is a scale of 1 through 4. At a 1, you're not being noticed. You may even be a welcomed guest. At a 4, you're either a wanted criminal or an open nuisance. You will get a lot of attention, and resource or time costs may go up!

Towns are safe from Monsters (usually), but they aren't always safe for your wallet! Moving around in town requires a toll, which is usually 10gp per town turn. This is an abstraction to represent your costs for the entire party staying the night, eating, maintaining gear and avoiding attention. You can move without spending this, but the heat may increase a level. 


### How do I move through the Dungeon?
You'll need a source of light! (A torch, candle, or lantern). If you're moving carefully, it makes it easier to spot dangers, but you move slowly and are on edge. Within every 6 turns, you'll need a turn to rest. You can push without resting, but there are consequences!

1. Wandering Monster check (if applicable)
2. What are you doing? Moving, listening, searching, opening doors?
3. Outcomes or rolls are decided.
4. End of turn, the dungeon tracker is updated. Light sources may burn out at the end of turns.

### How do I know how fast I can move?
Refer to [[Slot based encumbrance]]. In general-

**Light, Medium, or No armor**
- Half of your slots or less- 120' (40')
- More than half slots, less than full- 90' (30')
- Full slots- 60' (20')

**Heavy Armor**
- Half of your slots or less - 90' (30')
- More than half slots, less than full-60' (20')
- Full slots- 30' (10')

### How do I roll for a skill?
Unless you have a special percentile or X in 6 skill, you just describe what you are trying to do. 

If a roll is required, a roll based on a stat will be asked for. You just need to roll 1d20 and roll under your stat.
For example:

> Player: I'd like to seduce the Dragon
> DM: **Sigh** ... Roll a Charisma (CHA).
> Player: My CHA Score is 18, so I just need to roll an 18 or lower...I got a 16!
> DM: The Dragon has taken a liking to you...and has asked you to swear an oath to him. 
> Player: *Him?*
> DM: Yep.

### How do I roll for a special skill?
If you are trying to move silently or open a door for example, you should have a X in 6 chance or a percentile chance detailed in your character class section. These are special skills, and you should only need to use them if you are under time/combat pressure or there is other difficulty. Otherwise, you can just succeed. 

Examples:

You wish to open a stuck door. Your class gives you a 3 in 6 chance, so you roll a d6 and you get a 3. You succeed!

You wish to move silently, and your class gives you a 15 for success. You roll 1d100 and get an 27. You've failed!

In both of the above examples, failure doesn't mean the end. Failing to open a stuck door with a skill check doesn't mean that you can't spend the Dungeon turn hacking through the door with an Axe. Failing to move silently does not mean that you aren't being sneaky, you're just not *completely* silent. Your enemies may get a roll to hear or notice you.

### How do I attack?
Use your THAC0 (To hit armor class zero) score and refer to the attack matrix. For example, if you have a THAC0 of 19, and you're trying to hit a monster with an AC of 5, you need to roll a 14 or higher to hit. If you had a THAC0 of 18, you'd only need a 13 or higher to hit. 

Alternatively, you can take your THAC0-AC of the enemy to get the amount you need to roll.
