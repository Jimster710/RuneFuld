Skill checks are a roll under system, saves are a roll over system. That means for a dexterity check, you roll 1d20 and if you roll your stat level (say, 15) or lower, then you succeed. A natural 1 in this case would be a critical success. A natural 20 in this case would be a critical failure. 

For a save vs. wands score of 13, you'd need to roll a 13 or higher on the die to save. 

Because some checks are roll over, and some checks are roll under, it's always a good habit to call out any natural 1 or natural 20 that occurs. Either way something interesting should happen!


Skill checks are generally less common in B/X and AD&D. Auto successes are a lot more common if there is no time pressure or real risk of failure. 

Here's an example:

The party is travelling through the dungeon and comes across a craggy wall that they need to climb in order to continue. There don't seem to be monster noises particularly close. 

The thief can climb sheer surfaces with no hand holds-and this wall is craggy so it is easier. If the party spends a turn, the thief can simply succeed at climbing. They can then drop a rope down. With the help of the rope, the fighter could spend a turn driving in iron spikes and climbing up the wall. Everyone else can climb up within the same turn-but suddenly a few wandering goblins appear at the base of the wall as the magic-user is climbing!

Now the magic user is under pressure while climbing, but with a rope and iron spikes, they'll roll a dexterity or strength check with advantage.
